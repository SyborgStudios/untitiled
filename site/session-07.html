Anlage B – Dossier: Jakob (figuren.md)

Die Taufe (Autor, 15.07.2026): Er heißt Jakob – der Zweitgeborene im Schatten des Erstgeborenen (Jakob & Esau: der Nachgeborene, der den Platz des Ersten einnehmen soll und es nie kann). Der tote Bruder heißt Anton.

Gesetzt ist: 14, Kohortenkind, reine Haut – kurz vor dem Unfall gezeugt, geboren ins Jahr danach. Sohn von Lorenz, genannt „Lenz“, und einer Mutter, die seit der Unglücksnacht gezeichnet ist und in der Filmmitte stirbt. Der ältere Bruder starb in der Unglücksnacht – vor Jakobs Geburt. Der Empfindsame im Stammessystem, nicht der Anführer, aus armen Verhältnissen.

1. Der Nachgeborene

Jakob wurde in ein Haus geboren, das schon Trauer war, als er ankam: der Bruder tot, die Mutter gezeichnet, der Vater auf dem Weg nach innen. Er ist ein Nachgeborener im wörtlichsten Sinn – gezeugt vor dem Ende der Welt, geboren danach. Die Vergangenheit steht in jedem Zimmer, und niemand erklärt sie ihm: die Kleidung des Bruders im Schrank, das Schweigen des Vaters, die Male der Mutter. Jakob hat früh begriffen, dass der Frieden dieses Hauses eine einzige Bedingung hat: nicht fragen. Er hat die eine Frage, die alles sprengen würde, nie gestellt. Er weiß nicht, was sie ist. Er weiß nur, wo sie wohnt.

2. Der Bruder und die Jacke

Jakob kennt den Bruder nur als Loch in der Familie – und als Kleidung. Er trägt die Jacke des Toten: erst viel zu groß, jetzt fast passend. Er wächst in den Toten hinein, und niemand im Haus spricht darüber, dass die Jacke von Jahr zu Jahr besser sitzt.

Die Fieberverwechslung: In schlechten Phasen verwechselt die Mutter die Namen und nennt Jakob beim Namen des Bruders. Jakob korrigiert sie nicht. Er antwortet, als wäre er es – weil ihr Gesicht dann weich wird, und weil er ihr den Schmerz der Korrektur ersparen will. Liebe aus zweiter Hand: Jakob hat gelernt, sie anzunehmen, wie sie kommt, auch wenn sie einem anderen gilt. Sein leises Grundgefühl, das nie jemand ausspricht und vielleicht niemand denkt: dass der Falsche überlebt hat.

3. Der Reparierer

Jakob repariert. Er hat es vom Vater – nicht gezeigt bekommen, sondern abgeschaut: die Hände, die nachts Schuhe flicken. In einer Stadt, in der nichts mehr hergestellt wird, ist Reparieren die einzige Schöpfung, die es noch gibt. Die Gang duldet ihn nicht aus Gnade, sondern aus Nutzen: Er bringt Dinge wieder zum Laufen – Schlösser, Karren, Fallen. Seine Aufnahme in die Gang war der einzige gewaltfreie Eintritt, den sie je gewährt hat.

Anna katalogisiert die Welt; Jakob flickt sie. Und sein Verhängnis ist im Talent angelegt: Der Junge, der alles reparieren will, lebt zwischen Menschen, die nicht zu reparieren sind. Die Mutter nicht, der Vater nicht. Sein Bogen läuft vom Halten zum Tragen: lernen, dass manches nicht zu reparieren, nur zu tragen ist. (Das ist auch die Vergebungsthese des Films von seiner Seite: Vergebung ist keine Reparatur. Sie ist Tragen.)

4. Die Schuhe – der Briefwechsel

Mit etwa zehn hat Jakob nachts seine kaputten Schuhe vor das Bett gestellt – als Test. Am Morgen waren sie geflickt. Kein Wort, damals nicht und nie. Seither wiederholt er es. Und weil Schuhe nicht oft genug kaputtgehen: Manchmal lockert er selbst eine Naht, damit es etwas zu flicken gibt. Der Sohn erzeugt Gelegenheiten für die Liebe des Vaters, die sich nur über Dinge traut. Es ist der einzige Briefwechsel zwischen den beiden, und beide tun so, als gäbe es ihn nicht.

5. Die Mutter – und warum Male für Jakob banal sind

Jakob hat die Mutter nie gesund gekannt: „Mutter“ und „sterbend“ sind für ihn ein Wort. Er hat ihre Male gewaschen, gesalbt, gesehen, solange er denken kann. Daraus folgt etwas, das ihn von der ganzen Stadt unterscheidet, auch von Anna: Jakob hat keine Angst vor Malen. Für die Stadt sind sie Zeichen, für Anna sind sie Verdacht – für Jakob sind sie Haut, die Salbe braucht. Pflegealltag. (Konsequenz fürs Ende: Beim Abschied sieht der Zuschauer die Anklage, Anna sieht das Vielleicht – Jakob sieht nur seinen sterbenden Vater.)

Als die Mutter stirbt, trinkt der Vater. Jakob repariert – irgendwas, die ganze Nacht, etwas, das nicht kaputt war; er nimmt es auseinander und setzt es wieder zusammen. Zwei Männer, zwei Betäubungen.

6. Körper, Tempo, Sprache

Groß für vierzehn und wie entschuldigend darüber – er macht sich kleiner, als er ist (der Gegenentwurf zu Anna, die ihre Kleinheit nutzt). Langsame, genaue Hände; er spricht leise und zu Ende – keine abgebrochenen Sätze, das hat er nicht vom Vater. Höhenangst. Er hasst Höhe. Am Aussichtsort steht er trotzdem – er ist mit ihr hinaufgegangen, Schritt für Schritt, und hat nichts gesagt. Liebe als Überwindung, unangekündigt.

7. Was er liebt, was er will, was er braucht

Er liebt: den Moment, in dem ein Mechanismus wieder einrastet – dasselbe kleine Klicken, das Anna an der Gewissheit liebt; die Stimme der Mutter an guten Tagen; die Stunde, bevor die Gang wach ist. Und Anna: die Erste, die ihn ansieht, als wäre er nicht der Bruder-Ersatz, nicht der Sohn des Säufers, nicht der nützliche Reparierer – sondern einfach da.

Er will: dass alle bleiben. Der Junge, dem alles wegstirbt – Bruder vor der Geburt, Mutter in Zeitlupe, Vater in Abwesenheit –, will halten, flicken, versorgen.

Er braucht: die Erlaubnis loszulassen. Sein Bogen endet damit, dass er zum ersten Mal etwas wählt, statt etwas zu halten: Er geht mit Anna. Das Weggehen am Ende ist sein erstes selbstgewähltes Loslassen.

8. Widersprüche

  1. Er schämt sich des Vaters – und schlägt zu, wenn ein anderer über ihn spottet. Nur er darf sich schämen.
  2. Er repariert alles und hält sich selbst für unbegabt: Die Beweise seiner Buchhaltung laufen nur in eine Richtung – der Vater hat ihn nie gelobt.
  3. Der Sanfteste der Gang, ohne Angst vor Malen, vor Toten, vor Sterbenden – aber mit Angst vor Höhe und vor der einen Frage.
  4. Er trägt die Jacke eines Toten und ist der Lebendigste im Haus.

9. Anna – von seiner Seite

Bei ihr muss er niemanden verwalten: keine Pflege, keine Scham, keine Buchhaltung. Sie fragt nicht nach seiner Familie – er hält ihr Nichtfragen für Takt und weiß nicht, dass es Tarnung ist. Die Geheimnisse stabilisieren die Liebe, bevor sie sie bedrohen. Der Einband ihres Hefts löst sich; er näht ihn neu – ohne hineinzusehen. Sie hat es geprüft. Deshalb kann sie ihn lieben.

Und sein Ahnen: Was Jakob ahnt, ahnt er spät und ganz – in der Krise, spätestens beim Abschied, legen sich Annas Suche und sein Vater übereinander. Er spricht es nie aus. Er prüft es nie nach. Er geht mit ihr. Das Paar trägt zwei Ungesagte hinaus: Sie sagt nicht, was sie suchte. Er sagt nicht, was er ahnt.

Anlage D – Dossiers: Der Magnat & der Erzieher (figuren.md)

Der Magnat

Vorbemerkung: Im Film bleibt er Silhouette (die Verschwörung ist Bühne, nicht Zentrum): Plakate, Erzählungen, der Duft, vielleicht eine Stimme. Das Dossier kennt ihn trotzdem ganz – die Silhouette fällt nur richtig, wenn ein ganzer Mann sie wirft.

Der Dritte seines Namens. Erbe in dritter Generation. Der Großvater machte aus der Pflanze ein Geschäft, der Vater ein Imperium – er machte daraus eine Religion. Er ist der Erste seiner Linie, der die Pflanze nicht als Ware sieht, sondern als Wunder: der Ästhet als Endstufe des Kapitals. Damit sind es drei Männer, die dieselbe Pflanze lieben, auf drei Arten: Lenz mit den Händen, Jahn mit dem Kopf, der Magnat mit dem Begehren. Die Katastrophe entstand, als diese Lieben kollidierten.

Die Umarmungs-Sabotage. Er konnte Jahn nicht kaufen und die Bewegung nicht stoppen – also umarmte er sie tödlich. Wenn das Werk kommen muss, dann so schnell und so groß, dass es bricht. Kalkuliert hatte er eine Blamage – den Bankrott der Bewegung, ein stotterndes Werk. Die Katastrophe war größer als sein Plan. Er hat keine Sekunde getrauert. Er hat verwertet.

Die reinen Hände. Auch der Mord an Jahn trägt seine Handschrift, die keine ist: Ein lebender Jahn war eine tickende Bombe unter der Lüge; eigene Leute wären eine Spur gewesen. Also ließ er nur Informationen fließen – das Versteck, das weichgeschriebene Gutachten – und der Gerechteste der Bewegung wurde seine Waffe. Der Connaisseur hat das Objekt seiner Liebe eingemauert. Der ungestörte Schlaf. Was er liebt: die Essenz – das Reine, Konzentrierte, das Übrigbleibende. Was er will: wieder hinein, an die Quelle. Was er braucht: nichts – und das ist sein Horror. Der Erzieher

Der Bibliothekar. Er war Bibliothekar. Die Bücher der Stadt sind in den kalten Wintern verheizt worden, Band für Band, und er hat es mitangesehen und zuletzt selbst geheizt – man erfriert vor keiner Idee. Was er retten konnte, trägt er im Kopf: Er zitiert Sätze, deren Bücher nicht mehr existieren. Darum das Papier für Anna: Er gibt ihr das Kostbarste, was seine alte Welt hatte, und fragt nie, wofür – wer fragt, macht aus einem Geschenk eine Bedingung.

Warum er im Heim ist. Die Bücher sind verbrannt; die Kohorte ist, was der Stadt an unbeschriebenen Seiten bleibt. Er hütet die letzten leeren Blätter. Das ist seine stille Metaphysik, und er würde sie nie aussprechen – es wäre ihm peinlich, so etwas Großes über sich zu denken.

Das Aufnahme-Ding. Als Hüter der Papiere verwaltet er auch die Reste: was Kinder bei sich trugen, als sie gebracht wurden. Bei der namenlosen Vierjährigen von damals war etwas – er hat es aufbewahrt, alle zehn Jahre lang, ohne es deuten zu können (er hütet das Ding, nicht das Wissen). Irgendwann gibt er es ihr: „Das war bei dir, als du kamst.“ – Es ist ein kleines hölzernes Pferdchen. Die Vierjährige benannte das Furchtbarste nach ihrem Trostobjekt, das Liebste und das Schrecklichste in einem Wort. [Unter Vorbehalt: gekoppelt an die noch offene Taufe des Mals; beides wird am Treatment geprüft.]

Die offene Tür. In der Revolte hat er nichts angeführt und nichts verhindert. Aber Annas Weg hinaus hat er nicht versperrt – er sah sie gehen und hob nicht die Stimme. Der Hoffnungsvolle als stiller Komplize: Hoffnung, die man einsperrt, ist keine.

Humor: Er lacht über Witze aus Büchern, die keiner mehr kennt, und erklärt sie nie – „das würde sie ruinieren“. Die Kinder halten ihn für leicht verrückt und mögen ihn dafür. Spitzname der Kinder: „der Bleistift“ – lang, dünn, gibt Papier.

Referenz-Anker. Auster (die verschwindenden Wörter, die letzten Dinge); der eine anständige Beamte, den jeder Paranoia-Thriller der 70er hat – zu klein, um das System zu ändern, zu aufrecht, um es ganz zu werden.

I. The friendship – the hinge

Book and hand. The researcher came to the fields because his work needed someone who knew the plant not from papers but with his hands. The sentence. The movement’s slogan – “The fields belong to those who know them” (working version) – does not come from the politician. The authenticator. Why “most fervent comrade”? Because he was the argument. An educated man preaching communalization is suspect; a field worker standing beside him makes it true. He gave no speeches. His standing there was the speech.

The crack before the crack. The friendship did not die of the intrigue; it had grown thin before – of time and class, not of malice. The rise turned kitchen evenings into appointments; the office put people between them. And then the plant: At the topping-out ceremony the field worker knew with his hands what the researcher knew with his papers – too fast, too big. They fell silent at each other; their last conversation before the catastrophe was small talk. The friendship did not break – it was missed. That is the stuff both men’s remorse is later made of: There was no quarrel to take back. Only a conversation that never took place.

What the intrigue found. It invented nothing. It farmed what was there: the sting of the unattributed sentence, the missed conversation, one man’s name on the works of the disaster – and a man whose self-accusation was looking for a way out (→ II).

II. Dossier: J’s father – Lorenz, known as “Lenz”

The man from the fields. A family of field workers, for generations on land that belongs to the magnate. As a boy he learned to read the plant; as a man he rose – into the distillery where the harvest was made into the perfume. The good life: skilled work, a wife, two sons, a kitchen full of people. From the distillery he kept two things: the best nose in the city – and the knowledge of how to distill. The man who once distilled perfume today distills moonshine. The same skill, fallen.

The night of the disaster. When the works burned, he was not at home. He was out for the cause – for the friend: setting up chairs in a hall, posters, some small errand of the movement. The eldest son died while the father was helping politics. His private arithmetic ever since: I was with you instead of with him. His faith in the friend cost him his son – before the intrigue had said a single word. That is why the forged certainty later falls on such fertile ground: The intrigue offers his self-accusation a conversion. It wasn’t you – it was him. Whoever cannot forgive himself will take any address to which the guilt can be sent. His error is not stupidity; it is the soul’s self-defense.

The years of nursing. The wife: marked in the night, dying over years – he nurses her and drinks his way through the nursing. He is not a loud drunkard; he is a quiet, functional one. He does not drink to forget – he drinks against precision: Sober, the self-accusation does its arithmetic, and his nose reads his wife’s illness on her sleeves. The moonshine blurs the world just enough to bear it.

The road to the murder. The news that the friend is alive reaches him planted – the intrigue leads him, and he takes it for his own resourcefulness. What the news triggers in him is not hate at first; it is grief tipping over into meaning: If he is alive, then my son has a culprit. He goes there – not as executioner but as accuser: The gun is to force a confession, not to take a life. Then the door opens, and the hiding itself stands before him as proof: He let himself be celebrated as a dead man while our children burned. What happens then is not a plan, it is a collapse – the first shot for the friend, the second for the woman who steps between, panic. He never learned whether he wanted to kill. That is no exoneration. It is the opposite: You can distance yourself from a decision; not from a collapse.

The morning after. → Moved to the idea pile (parked July 15 – the author is unsure, possibly for the book). What stays set is the hands motif, unanchored: He cannot touch his son’s hands; J reads it as rejection. It is the opposite.

The ten years. The quiet drunkard, rarely there; at night he mends J’s shoes and places them by the bed – love through things, never through touch. The double shame: J is ashamed of the father, the father is ashamed before the son; the two directions of shame never meet. He does not search for it – he would have no right to find it. But he sees it in every girl and boy of the cohort. That is why he talks to Anna. A strange, quick-witted girl of the right age who listens: He confesses by proxy, to the child there might be, without ever knowing ██████████████████. The symmetry of not-knowing, from his side: She searches for a man with a mark; he searches for a child there might be; ████████████████████████

The decision. After his wife’s death nothing holds him – and the fast course begins, the marks multiply. Why the death run, why the supernova for the quietest man in the city? Because self-punishment seeks the form that costs the most: For a man who hid for ten years, visibility is the most expensive currency. To sign up means: to show yourself. Bare arms, all marks, all eyes. The run is his first honesty in ten years – and the only ecstasy a dried-out man has left.

What he loves, wants, needs. He loves: the smell of the fields at dawn (which no longer exists); his wife, to the last, against all reason of the body; J – mutely, through shoes. He wants: to pay. He needs: what he cannot give himself and dares not want – forgiveness. The film will not speak it for him. Perhaps, at the end, it stands beside him as a girl’s silence.

Contradictions. The most righteous man of the movement committed its greatest wrong. The finest nose in the city drinks its instrument to ruin. The father who loves his son cannot touch him. The hidden man chooses the most visible death. And: He is the wrong reader – the man who closed an unambiguous event (the friend lived in hiding) with the wrong meaning (betrayal instead of protection). He is thus the film’s built-in warning to its own audience, which at the end faces the same task: an unambiguous image, an open meaning. What the comrade got wrong, the audience must not repeat.

Reference anchors. Kohlhaas (the righteous man as destroyer), Johnny Smith with a forged vision, Leontes (the implanted doubt, the penance), Raskolnikov (the after; the confession to Sonya – here: to Anna).

III. Dossier: ██████████ – Jahn (the politician)

The researcher. No child of the fields and none of the villas – in between: an apothecary’s son, raised among drawers with Latin names. He came to the plant through chemistry and to the city through the plant. His discovery – that it can do energy, not just perfume – was first wonder, then politics: A city that draws its light from its own fields belongs to no one anymore. He loved in the plant what it can do; the magnate loved what it is. Two loves, one raw material – the war was built in.

The orator. His gift: He made politics out of people’s sentences – he listened, picked up, polished. His belief, almost religious: that language repairs. That for everything there is a phrasing that sets things right. It is a good belief, and it ruined him twice: once when he bowed – and once when he had to fall silent.

The bowing. The works came at the magnate’s tempo or not at all. There is an early report by him warning against it – and a later version he softened himself. His logic was the trap of the good: If I quit, they build it without me – worse. If my name is on it, they build it properly. His name as guarantee: the hubris of a man who believes in words. The intrigue later had to invent nothing – it took his own softened report as proof that he knew. They lied about him with his own truth. (And the terrible part: A remnant of it is true. He did know – with a researcher’s knowledge, as the friend knew with his hands’ knowledge. Both kept silent. The works bear his name rightly and wrongly at once.)

The hiding. For four years the orator is mute. A man of public speech, condemned to whispering – he, who believes in the repairing power of speaking, may not even contradict aloud while the city learns his name as a curse. What remains is lean and heavy: He knew of the friend’s dead son – a single sign, one line by any route might have… He never risked it; silence meant protecting his family. The omitted line is his second guilt, and for him it weighs more than the report.

The window game. For the child he invented a game: at the window, behind the curtain, giving the people outside names, making up stories about them, turning clouds into animals – silly, whispered, with suppressed giggling. No lessons, no intent: Here the deep, loving bond between ████ and her father is set – the best hour of the day, for both. That the child incidentally learned to watch is the narrator’s irony, not the father’s plan; the film makes nothing of it.

The name. His name is the loudest object in the film and belongs to no one anymore: It stands on the works (the ruin is named after him), it sits inside the official lie (the “terrorist”), the city curses with it. And the child who should bear it does not know it – in hiding, names were not spoken aloud. ████████████████████████████████████████████ (The father is everywhere as a name and nowhere as a person: the exact inversion of the comrade, who is there as a person and whose name stands on nothing – not even on his own sentence.)

The door. The night of the murder from inside: There is a knock, and he opens – why? Perhaps because four years of whispering wear a man down; perhaps because he recognized the voice. His last word was the friend’s nickname: “Lenz” – aloud. The first loud word in four years; ██████████████████████████████████. He did not die as a politician and not as a “terrorist” – he died as a man opening the door to a friend.

What he loved, wanted, needed. He loved: his wife, the only one allowed to laugh at his speeches (and she did); the wonder of research before it became politics; the child to whom he could show the world only as a window cutout. He wanted: the Later – the day when surfacing would no longer cost his family. He needed: what he could never give the friend and never received from him – the missed conversation. The two men died of the same omission, only in different nights.

Contradictions. The man of words who perishes of the unsaid line. The skeptic of the works whose name guaranteed them. The socialist living in hiding like a fugitive from his own people. The father who taught his child to hide – as a game, so it would not be a prison – and thereby gave it survival, in a night he did not survive.

Reference anchors. Joseph (the man believed dead, living hidden), Chinatown (guilt without a decision to act), Children of Men (the pledge of hope in the collapse), the Reichstag fire (his name inside the lie, faster than any scrutiny).

Attachment B – Dossier: Jakob (figuren.md)

The naming (author, July 15, 2026): His name is Jakob – the second-born in the shadow of the first-born (Jacob & Esau: the late-born who is meant to take the first one’s place and never can). The dead brother is called Anton.

Set: 14, a cohort child, clean skin – conceived shortly before the accident, born into the year after. Son of Lorenz, known as “Lenz”, and of a mother marked since the night of the disaster, who dies at the film’s middle. The elder brother died in the night of the disaster – before Jakob’s birth. The sensitive one in the tribal system, not the leader, from poor circumstances.

1. The late-born

Jakob was born into a house that was already grief when he arrived: the brother dead, the mother marked, the father on his way inward. He is late-born in the most literal sense – conceived before the end of the world, born after it. The past stands in every room, and no one explains it to him: the brother’s clothes in the wardrobe, the father’s silence, the mother’s marks. Jakob understood early that the peace of this house has a single condition: don’t ask. He has never asked the one question that would blow everything up. He does not know what it is. He only knows where it lives.

2. The brother and the jacket

Jakob knows the brother only as a hole in the family – and as clothing. He wears the dead boy’s jacket: first far too big, now almost fitting. He is growing into the dead, and no one in the house speaks of the jacket fitting better year by year.

The fever confusion: In bad phases the mother mixes up the names and calls Jakob by the brother’s name. Jakob does not correct her. He answers as if he were him – because her face softens then, and because he wants to spare her the pain of the correction. Love at second hand: Jakob has learned to accept it as it comes, even when it is meant for another. His quiet base feeling, which no one ever speaks and perhaps no one thinks: that the wrong one survived.

3. The mender

Jakob repairs. He has it from his father – not taught, but watched: the hands that mend shoes at night. In a city where nothing is produced anymore, repairing is the only creation left. The gang tolerates him not out of mercy but out of use: He makes things run again – locks, carts, traps. His admission into the gang was the only nonviolent entry it ever granted.

Anna catalogues the world; Jakob mends it. And his doom is built into the talent: The boy who wants to repair everything lives among people who cannot be repaired. Not the mother, not the father. His arc runs from holding to carrying: learning that some things cannot be repaired, only carried. (That is also the film’s thesis of forgiveness from his side: Forgiveness is not repair. It is carrying.)

4. The shoes – the correspondence

At about ten, Jakob put his broken shoes by the bed at night – as a test. In the morning they were mended. Not a word, not then and never. He has repeated it ever since. And because shoes do not break often enough: Sometimes he loosens a seam himself so there is something to mend. The son creates occasions for the father’s love, which only dares to happen through things. It is the only correspondence between the two, and both act as if it did not exist.

5. The mother – and why marks are banal to Jakob

Jakob has never known his mother healthy: “mother” and “dying” are one word to him. He has washed her marks, salved them, seen them for as long as he can think. From this follows something that sets him apart from the whole city, even from Anna: Jakob is not afraid of marks. To the city they are signs, to Anna they are suspicion – to Jakob they are skin that needs salve. Everyday care. (Consequence for the ending: At the farewell the audience sees the accusation, Anna sees the perhaps – Jakob sees only his dying father.)

When the mother dies, the father drinks. Jakob repairs – something, all night, something that was not broken; he takes it apart and puts it back together. Two men, two anesthesias.

6. Body, tempo, speech

Tall for fourteen and as if apologizing for it – he makes himself smaller than he is (the counter-design to Anna, who uses her smallness). Slow, precise hands; he speaks quietly and to the end – no broken-off sentences; that he does not have from his father. Fear of heights. He hates heights. At the lookout he stands anyway – he went up with her, step by step, and said nothing. Love as overcoming, unannounced.

7. What he loves, wants, needs

He loves: the moment a mechanism clicks back into place – the same small click Anna loves about certainty; his mother’s voice on good days; the hour before the gang wakes. And Anna: the first person who looks at him as if he were not the brother-substitute, not the drunkard’s son, not the useful mender – but simply there.

He wants: everyone to stay. The boy from whom everything dies away – brother before his birth, mother in slow motion, father in absence – wants to hold, mend, provide.

He needs: permission to let go. His arc ends with him choosing something for the first time instead of holding something: He goes with Anna. The leaving at the end is his first self-chosen letting-go.

8. Contradictions

  1. He is ashamed of his father – and strikes when another mocks him. Only he is allowed to be ashamed.
  2. He repairs everything and considers himself untalented: The evidence of his bookkeeping runs in one direction only – the father never praised him.
  3. The gentlest of the gang, unafraid of marks, of the dead, of the dying – but afraid of heights and of the one question.
  4. He wears a dead boy’s jacket and is the most alive person in the house.

9. Anna – from his side

With her he has to manage no one: no nursing, no shame, no bookkeeping. She does not ask about his family – he takes her not-asking for tact and does not know it is camouflage. The secrets stabilize the love before they threaten it. The binding of her notebook comes loose; he sews it anew – without looking inside. She checked. That is why she can love him.

And his sensing: What Jakob senses, he senses late and whole – in the crisis, at the farewell at the latest, Anna’s search and his father settle over each other. He never speaks it. He never verifies it. He goes with her. The pair carries two unsaid things out: She does not say what she was searching for. He does not say what he senses.

Reference anchors. Florizel (the son who lives the reconciliation the old were not capable of); The Ice Storm for the tone of his scenes; and Donald’s line is literal with him: He is what he loves – repairable things in an irreparable world.

Attachment C – Double dossier: The mothers (figuren.md)

Developed together like the fathers – the women mirror each other too: One lives on as story, the other as care. One died in a second and remained whole in memory; the other has been dying for fourteen years and becomes daily less whole.

I. The mirroring – the hinge

Word and body. ████ mother leaves behind language: a story, a promise, a place. Jakob’s mother leaves behind body: care, marks, the everyday. The children inherit accordingly – ██████████████████████████; Jakob a responsibility he was never allowed to hand over.

The women knew each other. From the kitchen era of the friendship: While the men shared movement and stage, the women shared the evenings – and they understood each other faster than the men grew formal. Lenz’s knowledge of the pregnancy comes from the women’s intimacy – his wife knew it first, from the friend herself. The knowledge that hunts him for ten years came into the house as kitchen whispering.

The mothers’ marks – and the children’s relations to marks. Both women are marked. But they carry it in opposite ways: ████ mother hid her marks from the child – the child saw them a single time, while washing, and the mother startled and covered herself. So ████ learned: Marks are what one hides – the uncanny. Jakob’s mother could hide nothing – her marks needed care, and the boy washed them. So Jakob learned: Marks are skin that needs salve. The children’s opposite relations to the marks are the mothers’ inheritance.

II. Dossier 5: ████ mother

The laugh in the wrong place. At one of his first public speeches a woman laughed out loud at a spot that was not meant to be funny – she had heard the involuntary comedy of a phrase everyone else swallowed devoutly. He looked for her after the speech. She remained the only one allowed to laugh at his speeches – she had claimed that office in the first minute. (Her humor is her nature: She hears what sentences really say.)

The storyteller. A teacher – a woman who could tell children stories before she had one of her own. The orator and the storyteller: He spoke to a thousand people, she to thirty children, and she never believed his work was greater for it. The lookout is hers – her place since her own childhood; she showed it to Jahn, not the other way round. That she told him of the pregnancy there was no coincidence: She said the most important sentence of her life at her own place. (And the children find each other a generation later at the mother’s place.)

The hiding – the one who went out. For four years she was the connection to the world: Only he had been declared dead; hardly anyone knew her face. She went to trade, to fetch, to listen – wrapped like all the marked, inconspicuous among the inconspicuous. The orator could not even whisper without betraying himself; she carried the family through the years. [Internal and hazy: The trail that betrayed the hiding place was hers – without any guilt. The woman who did everything right for four years was at some point simply seen. The intrigue only had to follow. The film never explains this; we have to know it.]

The story as legacy. The mother’s story of the lookout was her answer to the hiding’s greatest pain: that the child does not know the world. She gave ████ a place the child had never set foot in – and a promise: When it is all over, we will go there. She could not keep it. ████████████████████████████████████████████████ The perfume remnant was her thimbleful of vanity from the former life – the only thing she took with her out of the world.

Her death. She stepped between (the second shot). From her side it is no reflex but her whole nature in one motion: She was a shield from beginning to end – first with the womb (██████████, because the mother’s body took the poison), then with the story (which replaced the world for the child), last with her body before the gun.

The name. In the film she remains nameless – “the mother”. She exists as voice, story, scent; ██████████████████████████ (in hiding, names were not spoken aloud – as with the father). The namelessness is no lack; it is the condition of the inheritance.

III. Dossier 6: Jakob’s mother – Ruth

The woman with the laugh. A harvest worker from the fields, like Lenz – that is where they found each other: He made a punch line, she laughed the loudest, and you could hear her laugh two houses away. The good life was her work: the kitchen full of people, in which the men’s friendship had its best years, was her kitchen. Lenz supplied the jokes; she was the resonance chamber in which they grew big.

The night. Anton dies; she is marked; Jakob is in her belly. The woman whose laugh could be heard two houses away comes out of this night quiet – and grows quieter for fourteen years.

She stayed. Medically, her slow course is the poison’s arbitrariness. But internally we tell it differently: She did not die for fourteen years – she stayed for fourteen years. Because of Jakob. She did not want to leave the late-born alone with a dead brother and a drinking father, and her body held to that will as long as it could. No one in the film says it; her death at the film’s middle comes when Jakob is fourteen – old enough. (Perhaps she knows it before he does.)

The not-asking – her last care. She does not know what Lenz did. But she has known since that time that something is – that something broke in her husband that was not grief but guilt. She decided never to ask: Her husband was broken twice; for a third truth she had no strength – and no use. She dies without having asked. It is her last act of care and her hardest achievement. Jakob has the not-asking from her, not from his father. (The spouses’ symmetry: He reckons with a child, she reckons with a deed. Both carry an unasked question to the end.)

The fever confusion, from her side. On bad days Anton lives in her, and Jakob answers in his place. On good days she knows exactly who stands before her – and you can tell the good days by her making jokes: coarse field jokes, jokes about her own dying, which Jakob cannot bear and Lenz needs. [A fine point: Whether she ever noticed that Jakob plays the brother to spare her the correction – and let him, to spare him the pain: the double sparing as her quietest chapter. The film never shows it; the dossier may sense it.]

Her death. The tipping point: Nothing holds Lenz anymore; Jakob spends a night repairing something that was not broken. Her dying at the film’s middle is the moment both men choose their anesthesia – and the proof that the film never used her dying as a plot clock: She was a person, not a deadline.

The name. Named: Ruth – the gleaner of the fields, the biblical loyalty (“Whither thou goest, I will go”). A woman from the fields who stayed.

Attachment D – Dossiers: The magnate & the caretaker (figuren.md)

The magnate

Preliminary note: In the film he remains a silhouette (the conspiracy is stage, not center): posters, told stories, the perfume, perhaps a voice. The dossier knows him whole nonetheless – the silhouette only falls right when a whole man casts it.

The third of his name. An heir in the third generation. The grandfather made a business out of the plant, the father an empire – he made a religion of it. He is the first of his line who sees the plant not as goods but as a wonder: the aesthete as the final stage of capital. That makes three men who love the same plant, in three ways: Lenz with his hands, Jahn with his head, the magnate with desire. The catastrophe arose when these loves collided.

The embrace sabotage. He could not buy Jahn and could not stop the movement – so he embraced it fatally. If the works must come, then so fast and so big that they break. He financed generously, urged publicly, praised Jahn to the skies, and gave the city deadlines like festivals – whoever brakes is an enemy of the people. Thus he forced the skeptic into bowing without a single threat: He killed the city with enthusiasm, not with bombs. What he had calculated was an embarrassment – the movement’s bankruptcy, a stuttering plant. The catastrophe was bigger than his plan. He did not mourn for a second. He put it to use.

The clean hands. The murder of Jahn also bears his signature, which is none: A living Jahn was a ticking bomb under the lie; his own people would have been a trail. So he only let information flow – the hiding place, the softened report – and the most righteous man of the movement became his weapon. The deed even confirmed the lie: See, these fanatics murder one another. He ordered nothing. His hands are clean in a way that is dirtier than blood.

The present. He is alive – outside, old, behind walls of taste. And he built his punishment himself: The sealing-off that protects his lie locks him away from the only source – the plant keeps growing inside, wild, unreachable, and his stock of essence is running out. The connoisseur has walled in the object of his love. [Small, as a marginal image: There is smuggling across the barrier – harvesting inside, buying outside; perhaps one of the gangs’ businesses. The children of the catastrophe harvest the plant for the man who caused it, and no one inside knows. In the film at most an image, never an explanation.]

The untroubled sleep. What he loves: the essence – the pure, the concentrated, that which remains. What he wants: back in, to the source. What he needs: nothing – and that is his horror. Lenz breaks on guilt, Jahn died of two omissions; the magnate sleeps well. The evil of this film is no hatred and no plan – it is untroubled sleep. (Forgiveness is only a question where someone needs it.)

Humor: the charm of power – obliging, self-deprecating in small things, the most dangerous kind. He jokes about himself to hide bigger things; his laughter costs nothing and buys everything.

No name in the film. Jahn’s name is the film’s name-object; the magnate needs no second one. What remains visible of him is a brand – the perfume’s name on weathered posters. The dying call him “the old man”.

Reference anchors. Noah Cross (Chinatown), Perfume as a warning, the silphium of Cyrene (the plant that made its city rich and perished with it), the beneficiary of the Reichstag fire.

The caretaker

The librarian. He was a librarian. The city’s books were burned for heat in the cold winters, volume by volume, and he watched it and in the end stoked the fire himself – no idea keeps you from freezing. What he could save, he carries in his head: He quotes sentences whose books no longer exist. Hence the paper for Anna: He gives her the most precious thing his old world had, and never asks what for – whoever asks turns a gift into a condition.

Why he is in the home. The books are burned; the cohort is what the city has left in unwritten pages. He guards the last blank sheets. That is his quiet metaphysics, and he would never say it aloud – he would be embarrassed to think something so grand about himself.

The intake object. As keeper of the papers he also manages the remnants: what children carried when they were brought. With the nameless four-year-old of back then there was something – he has kept it, all ten years, without being able to interpret it (he guards the object, not the knowledge). At some point he gives it to her: “This was with you when you came.” – It is a small wooden horse. The four-year-old named the most terrible thing after her comfort object, the dearest and the most dreadful in one word. [With reservation: coupled to the still-open naming of the mark; both will be tested against the treatment.]

The open door. In the revolt he led nothing and prevented nothing. But he did not bar Anna’s way out – he saw her go and did not raise his voice. The hopeful one as quiet accomplice: Hope you lock up is none.

Humor: He laughs at jokes from books no one knows anymore and never explains them – “that would ruin them”. The children think him slightly mad and like him for it. The children’s nickname: “the Pencil” – long, thin, gives paper.

Reference anchors. Auster (the vanishing words, the last things); the one decent official every seventies paranoia thriller has – too small to change the system, too upright to become it entirely.

Attachment E – Addendum: The characters’ humor (figuren.md)

Setting by the author: The characters have humor – fatalistic, morbid if need be, but they make each other laugh and smile. Otherwise it all becomes too bleak. (Adopted as principle 12 of the working principles.)

  • Anna: dry observer’s humor. She says true things at the most wrong moment – her punch line is always an exact observation, delivered deadpan. And she can imitate: gang leaders, caretakers, anyone. She does it only for Jakob. The imitating is her tenderness.
  • Jakob: quiet understatement and absurd repairs – he solemnly “repairs” things for her that never worked, and hands them over with a straight face. He laughs last and longest: His laughter must first click into place like a mechanism, then the whole body laughs – and he is instantly embarrassed.
  • Lenz: the gallows humor of the dying – historically attested (the liquidators’ jokes). The field worker could always do punch lines: Jahn gave the speeches, Lenz made the jokes. Today he jokes about his own marks and his own decline, with the old nose’s precision – and makes Anna, of all people, smile. The drunkard’s confessions are no litany: They are the numbers of a storyteller who has found his last audience. (That makes them bearable – and much sadder.)
  • Jahn: his humor was publicly rationed – a politician may smile, not be silly. In private he was childish: The window game (silly, whispered, with suppressed giggling) is his humor, and ████████████████████████████. And the friendship had a core of humor: Lenz was the only one with whom Jahn could laugh out loud. The man who supplied the orator’s punch lines is what he misses most in hiding.
  • The city: gallows humor as the culture of the zone – attested in reality (the joke culture after Chernobyl). Belongs in the background: graffiti on the barrier, the children’s nicknames for the drops, for the caretakers, for death. The zone laughs quietly at itself.

Test question for treatment and draft, at every sequence of scenes: When did anyone last laugh?
